## 5. THE CAMPAIGN CONCEPT (client's, originally seeded by Azam)

Creators post 2-3 outfit options and invite their audience to use StyleScore to vote or give
feedback. The creator's audience becomes the acquisition engine, and the activity itself
demonstrates the core product behaviour. Much stronger than "download StyleScore".

**Azam's three additions to make it convert:**
1. The creator must NOT reveal their own pick in the caption. The answer lives inside the app.
2. A deadline. "Voting closes tonight" moves people now instead of later.
3. A reveal follow-up post showing the winner and the vote split. It rewards voters and produces
   a second piece of content from the same creator at no extra cost.

**Honest risk flagged to the client:** installing an app just to vote is much higher friction
than a link click. Mitigations: the creator shows the app on screen so it feels familiar, and a
unique tracking link or code per creator so we can see which creators actually convert.
**Without per-creator attribution we cannot separate good creators from weak ones.**

The brief needs a fallback (include friends' votes, or show whatever feedback exists).

## 12. SUCCESS METRICS ALREADY COMMITTED TO (the client asked how success is measured)

**Layer 1, what Azam owns and reports weekly:** creators contacted, positive reply rate,
creators onboarded, content delivered on time, cost per delivered video including rights.

**Layer 2, what the content produces:** installs per creator via unique link or code,
activation rate (how many of those installs post a first look or get a first reaction),
**cost per activated user rather than cost per install**, and the performance of each video
once budget is put behind it.

Splitting these two layers was deliberate. It prevents the client from later blaming Azam
for install numbers that depend on the product and the offer, not on the outreach.

## 13. CONTENT IDEAS ALREADY SENT TO THE CLIENT (kept for reuse)

1. **The group chat problem.** Creator sends five outfit photos to a group chat, gets no replies,
   deadline approaching. Then StyleScore, instant emoji reactions, decision made. The product's
   real enemy is the unanswered group chat.
2. **Cost per wear.** The closet tracks brand, purchase price and last-worn date, so a creator
   calculating cost per wear on a splurge item is native content and shows a feature no styling
   app usually has.
3. **Accidental outfit repeat.** "I wore this to the same friend group twice." Last-worn tracking
   solves it. Works as a fast 15-second video.
4. **Get ready with me, but the audience decides.** The creator narrows to two options and sends
   viewers into the app to vote. This became the client's chosen campaign.
5. **Roommate fit check.** For the college segment, roommates rating each other's outfits in the
   app is a natural duet and stitch format.

## 14. CREATOR SOURCING CRITERIA (for the 15-creator sample list, section 10)

1. **Platform:** TikTok and Instagram, both briefs list hashtags for both.
2. **Tier:** nano (~1K-10K followers) and micro (~10K-100K followers), per section 1.
3. **Location:** US-based creator AND majority-US audience (check the audience, not just the
   creator's own location).
4. **Content niche, kept broad:** GRWM, outfit/fit check, everyday style, closet content,
   style-focused lifestyle creators. Do not narrow to "fashion influencer" only, the brand's own
   testimonials range from an 18-year-old to a 30-year-old new mom (section 3).
5. **Engagement rate over follower count:** roughly 5%+ for nano, 2-4%+ for micro, as a starting
   bar. Watch for bot-inflated followers (comment ratio and comment quality are the tell).
6. **Audience demographics:** check age skew (avoid a heavily under-18 audience, FTC/disclosure
   risk), gender skew can be mixed, the app is not gender-specific.
7. **Content format match:** already camera-facing, talking-style content. A strong bonus is
   creators who already show friends or roommates in their content, since both briefs lean on a
   "real friends disagreeing" dynamic (see the roommate fit check idea above).
8. **Posting consistency:** active in the last 30 days, regular cadence, no dead accounts.
9. **Brand safety:** no controversial or judgmental tone, briefs are explicit that StyleScore is
   "confidence over perfection, not judgment or comparison."
10. **Open to paid collabs:** since the app is free, a real fee is required to hit decent
    acceptance (section 6), so prioritize creators with a track record of small paid brand
    collabs, they already know how to negotiate and disclose.

**Still blocking real targeting precision:** iOS-only vs. also-Android is unconfirmed (section
3). Once known, weight sourcing toward creators whose audience device split favors whichever
platform(s) the app actually supports.
